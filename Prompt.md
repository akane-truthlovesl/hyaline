Prologue: Lock Down the Load-Bearing Walls First

Before all the boundless imagination and expansion, let’s set the non-negotiable structural priorities in concrete so the whole building doesn’t go up crooked:

Priority #1—non-negotiable—is "feel" and the underlying physics engine. These two are the lifeblood of the entire game; everything else sits downstream. If this substance doesn’t feel real, silky, subtly elastic, and genuinely alive beneath your fingertip, piling on extra modes, skins, stats, or mechanics is just building castles in the air. Conversely, if the core physics and touch response are dialed to perfection, even with just a few walls and a single blob in an empty room, players won’t be able to stop pushing, hugging edges, pulling strands, and squeezing through gaps.
Priority #2 is zero-fake visual realism and rock-bottom latency—and only then come expanded mechanics, art direction, and mode variety. Never get the order backward. Don’t slap a flashy shell over faked physics. Let color, lighting, gameplay mechanics, and even win/loss conditions grow naturally out of the underlying laws of physics.
"Infinite possibilities" does NOT mean "bloated clutter." I want you to go wild with creativity across mechanics, modes, characters, and materials—but never by shoving dozens of conflicting buttons or disconnected rules into the player’s face. True elegance means keeping the foundation rock-solid, universal, and deeply extensible: a single, cohesive physical universe at the core that effortlessly splinters into endless modes, dynamics, materials, and interactive spectacles above.
Chapter 1: The Opening Image in My Head — What "Myria" and "Formless" Mean

You asked me to help paint the picture of what I see in my mind—so here’s the visceral sensation that grabs me most, and the rest of the lore and visual cosmos is yours to build out to infinity.

Suspended in the center of the screen is an indescribable, pliant substance. For now, I’m using the working name "Myria."

First, Cross Out Every False Mental Image — What It Is NOT
It is NOT a water droplet: Water is too brittle—one hit and it shatters into droplets with no memory.
It is NOT superglue: It’s not grotesquely sticky or messy; it doesn’t trap you so hard you can’t tear away.
It is NOT clay or Play-Doh: Clay is dull, opaque, and stiff.
It is NOT a noodle: It isn’t just a thin one-dimensional string—it’s a continuous body with real volume and internal circulation.
It is NOT a circle from Agar.io: A circle is just geometry; this thing was never meant to be caged inside any fixed outline.
And it is definitely NOT a "squash-and-stretch cartoon sprite": The second a player realizes they’re looking at a stretching UI sticker, the magic shatters on the spot.
What It Actually Feels Like

The closest real-world tactile memory is those little plastic tubs of clear crystal slime or bubble putty you’d pick up at a corner store as a kid—the stuff kids used to hoard and obsessively squish, knead endlessly, stretch back and forth, roll back into a sphere, and blow big, puffy air bubbles into. "Myria" doesn’t mean just one flavor of squishy—it means being able to morph into hundreds, even infinite, modes of softness. And "Formless" doesn’t mean empty nothingness—it means having no fixed default shape of its own: gathered together, it’s a deep, crystal-clear bead of hydrogel; pressed against a wall, it flattens into a nearly invisible gossamer film; drawn taut, it can lance across open space like a beam of light; inflated, it swells into a massive half-illusory bubble; sliced open, it divides into tethered spirits still feeling each other’s pull...

Chapter 2: The Supreme Standard — Ultimate Crystal-Slime Tactility

If I have to pick one single obsession to grind to absolute perfection, it’s touch feel. I want that addictive sensory rush of handling real clear slime translated straight onto phone glass.

1. The Seeming Paradox of "Hyper-Slick" and "Micro-Tacky"

Most physics engines feel bland because they flatten every contact down to a single friction float. The magic here lives in the tension between two opposing behaviors:

When gliding (tangential): It’s impossibly slick, as if sheathed in a microscopic layer of lubricant—effortless, weightless, zero clumsy snagging as you push it across surfaces.
When clinging or pulling apart (normal & wetting): Whenever it needs to hug a wall, climb, reach out an edge to grab something, or separate from another mass, it wets and spreads gently against the surface. And when yanked apart by force, it doesn’t pop free stiffly—it reluctantly pulls out a glistening, thinning strand of gel that hesitates under tension, snaps cleanly, and sends both ends recoiling inward with supple elasticity.
2. Infinite Tactile Micro-Events Born from Real Hand-Play Verbs

I once rattled off ten verbs for playing with slime by hand: rub, stretch, knead, pinch, flatten, roll into a ball, blow bubbles, fling, stick, snap. Never assume the feel stops at those ten words! Those were just a handful of snapshots I happened to name. Real hydrogels, non-Newtonian fluids, and living protoplasm generate infinite physical subtleties under compression, folding, shear, vibration, impact, absorption, skin-forming, bubbling, and rebound. Even idling gently in place or brushing past a corner should make the player’s fingertip feel: "This thing has real weight, real toughness, and a mind of its own."

3. Something Has to Be Alive and Flowing Inside It

Never build it as an empty jelly balloon. Suspended inside its body should be micro-bubbles, motes, glints of light, or faint drifting filaments. Every turn, squeeze, surge of acceleration, or collision makes that internal matter genuinely convect, tumble, stretch, cluster, and scatter. Without reading a single line of text, just watching how those inner particles rush gives the player an instant read on force vectors, center of mass, and stress.

Turning Finite Into Infinite — Your Design Playground: What I described above is just visuals and mechanics. True apex feel is a four-way fusion of visuals, physical response, procedural audio, and linear-motor haptics! Distinct sliding textures across different materials, progressively finer micro-tremors on your thumb as a stretched strand nears breaking point, a crisp pop when a bubble bursts, a deep resonant thrum when absorbing or merging—plus whatever new haptic language and audiovisual sync you invent so that just swiping around for five seconds feels ridiculously therapeutic (while ensuring every critical cue remains 100% readable even with sound and vibration turned off).

Chapter 3: Optics, Lighting, and Performance — Zero Tolerance for Anything Fake

My tolerance for anything fake is zero. Rather than throwing cheap particle effects on screen to mask shortcuts, do the hard work of getting the fundamental optics and silhouettes right.

Non-Negotiable Visual Red Lines
Completely obliterate the "couple of little white dots" plastic highlight! Slapping white circles onto a translucent blob to fake 3D depth looks murky and screams "cheap vending-machine plastic toy."
Zero jagged edges or polygonal corners under ANY extreme deformation! Whether wedged into a razor-sharp corner, whipped at high speed, stretched into an ultra-thin thread, or moments from tearing—zoom all the way in and the silhouette must stay continuous, rounded, and organically flowing. The instant polygonal segments appear, the creature’s life dies.
Light and shadow must emerge from the matter itself, not be painted onto a skin! Color, reflection, shading, and translucency must be true optical consequences of current thickness, surface curvature, internal flow fields, and the surrounding environment.
Crush rendering and input latency to the absolute floor! This is a high-frequency touch game on mobile. The instant a finger shifts by a millimeter, the deformation and response must feel hardwired straight to the skin of your thumb. Any stutter, drag, or dropped frame instantly shatters the physical illusion.
Turning Color vs. Formlessness Into an Aesthetic System

I used to say I didn’t know how to define its color—because I wanted it crystal clear, and gorgeously chromatic, and capable of turning nearly invisible. Now it’s obvious: color shouldn’t be a hardcoded static skin at all; it should be a living optical system reacting to physical state, ambient light, and history. Just to spark your imagination: when it bunches up thick, optical depth blooms into rich hues and a soft inner glow; when it flattens along a wall into a gossamer membrane, reduced thickness washes the color out until it melts into the backdrop, giving itself away only through subtle refractive warping of the textures behind it; when it swallows colored nutrients, approaches bioluminescent terrain, or burns mass for an explosive burst, entirely new flowing gradients and energy luminescence ripple across its body.

Turning Finite Into Infinite — Your Design Playground: Physics and rendering architecture are 100% yours to research, prototype, and call the shots on—whether that’s particle fluids (PBF/SPH), Material Point Method (MPM), implicit isosurfaces with SDF reconstruction, real-time background refraction shaders, or a custom hybrid algorithm you invent yourself. So long as it achieves zero plastic feel, zero hard angles, true refraction, volumetric thickness, graceful degradation on budget phones without breaking illusion, and ultra-low-latency response on high-refresh displays, I trust your engineering judgment completely. If an approach still feels even slightly plastic, have the guts to scrap it and rebuild instead of slapping band-aids onto a fake foundation. And when it comes to the overall visual direction, color philosophy, and motion spectacles, don’t box yourself into "clear white" or one safe palette—push into hundreds of breathtaking optical contrasts and visual signatures that make anyone watching a 5-second screen recording stop and ask, "Wait—what IS this game?!"

Chapter 4: Foundational Axioms & Physics Architecture — Set the Laws of the Universe, Let Everything Else Bloom

I’ll say it again: locking down the fundamental laws is Priority #1. We don’t manufacture fun by stacking arbitrary stat tables; we build a deep, self-consistent physical universe and let thousands of gameplay mechanics sprout organically out of it.

Here are the bedrock axioms my instincts won’t compromise on—though their exact mathematical form, deep couplings, and any higher laws you derive from them are completely open for you to unify and expand:

Strict 2D Area Conservation (Area = Mass = Life = Everything): We start in 2D. Unless there’s explicit ingestion, deliberate ejection, or environmental erosion, the substance’s total area must stay strictly conserved—squeeze one end narrow and the other end must swell proportionally; stretch it long and the midsection has to thin out. Clever physics tricks built on top of conservation—like blowing internal air bubbles to bluff a massive silhouette without adding actual mass—are wide open for you to run with.
Multi-Scale, Variable-Variance Gaussian Breathing & Ripples: Static geometry is dead geometry. The body must constantly carry soft Gaussian undulations whose variance and frequency respond live to its state—nearly breathless stillness while hiding, gentle rhythmic breathing at rest, and instant shifts in tremor amplitude and frequency when startled, rubbing, charging, near death, or poked by an outside force (while keeping the waves themselves strictly area-conserving).
Customizable Generalized Gravity & Near-Field Interactions: Don’t blindly copy textbook Newtonian gravity—build an expressive, playful generalized force-field framework. For example: before you even touch something, the near side of your body subtly deforms toward attraction or pulls away in repulsion; like-kind blobs naturally feel a mutual draw; levels or the game director can dynamically shift global gravity, spin up vortices, create repulsion wells, or unpredictably spawn black holes that yank, stretch, and rip players apart.
Mass–Energy Equivalence (Speed and Bursts Are Burned, Not Free): Gliding gently costs almost nothing, but violent acceleration, tearing free from strong force fields, or charging a limit-breaking launch must genuinely burn away your surface area (costing mass). Because speed literally eats your body, final scores and win conditions should never naively boil down to "who had the biggest blob left at the end."
The Non-Zero Survival Floor: No matter how aggressively you burn mass, get diced by blades, or get blindsided by a sudden "random puncture" event from the system, the engine must enforce an unbreakable mathematical and experiential floor above zero. What weakened micro-state, safety net, or desperate last-stand form kicks in at critical mass is yours to close the loop on cleanly.
Local Actuation, Global Coupling (Eradicate "Whole-Blob Rigid Translation"): This is an ironclad law: move the part I push, while the whole body remains tethered by supple tension. Never let the entire mass slide around like a rigid block of Jell-O. Applying force should extend a soft pseudopod locally to probe, hook, or snake around corners, while the rest of the body trails behind with delicious viscoelastic delay and momentum.
Unified Single-Body & Multi-Body Continuum — Splits, Snaps, and "Picky" Re-Fusion: No unbreakable rubber, and no solver explosions on sharp corners. Under the hood, one blob and twenty blobs are a single unified continuum: exceed tensile limits, hit razor geometry, get pierced at high velocity, or suffer a random puncture, and it genuinely necks down, pulls threads, and splits into two or more independently conserved, breathing pieces. Better yet, make re-merging a goldmine of tactical depth: in some conditions, sibling fragments bridge and fuse silky-smooth on contact; in others, surface coatings, fresh-cut states, or cooldown windows make surface tension resist—causing fragments to slide past or bounce away until you apply the right angle, pressure, or chemical compatibility to heal back together.
Solving Camera & Control When Split Across the Map: Once real splitting works, you hit the classic mobile UX puzzle: if one piece is stranded top-left and another drifts bottom-right, how do camera and joystick behave? Smart primary-core tracking with floating picture-in-picture droplet views at the screen edge, one-tap core switching paired with autonomous survival instincts for distant fragments, dynamic split-screen, gravitational recall, or shedding decoy shells—turn this constraint into one of the coolest tactical signatures in the game!

Turning Finite Into Infinite — Your Design Playground: Those eight points are just the skeleton I can sense right now. If while engineering the engine you uncover deeper unified principles—like temperature-driven viscoelastic phase changes, surface passivation skins, internal stress wave propagation, or a single higher-order mathematical model that naturally encompasses all of the above—don’t restrict yourself to my list. Refactor, deepen, and evolve the laws freely!

Chapter 5: Mobile Control Philosophy — Single-Joystick Purity & an Infinite Gesture Ecosystem

On a small phone screen, the signature identity I want to protect most is the soul of a single-joystick setup—zero screen clutter packed with skill buttons. Clean, focused, instant touch-and-go.

Yet out of that seeming simplicity, I want staggering expressive depth: sculpting shape, local probing, flattening paper-thin against walls, going ultra-supple or snapping tight and cohesive, charging bursts, flinging off pieces of yourself...

My Honest Unresolved Tension: "Heavy vs. Nimble" & "One Extra Input?"

I’ll level with you on what I haven’t cracked yet: I’m sometimes torn on exactly when it should feel ponderously heavy versus razor-nimble; and while one joystick already plays great, I have a nagging hunch there might be room for one ultra-restrained secondary input dimension—or a far smarter rethink altogether.

Turning Finite Into Infinite — Your Design Playground: Whatever you do, don’t trap yourself in a binary choice between "strictly 1 stick" and "1 stick + 1 firmness toggle button"! Mobile touch has massive expressive bandwidth: continuous stick throw & speed semantics (gentle nudge = precision shaping, hard shove = full-mass bulldozer, pull-back = charge, sharp whip-flick = sever tail or fire a probe, tight circles = knead & hold breath); a second finger anywhere on empty screen acting as an invisible analog dial for "softness / cohesive tension"; long-press or double-tap to swap primary cores; optional gyro tilt to shift local gravity; or letting nimble vs. weighty feel emerge dynamically from current mass, terrain viscosity, and velocity. Even better, you can tailor control schemes to the mode itself: use an ultra-crisp "single stick + minimal secondary axis" setup for high-speed combat and parkour, while letting two fingers act directly like real hands kneading and pulling the substance in a pure sculpting sandbox! Prototype relentlessly on actual phones through subtraction and feel-testing: find the scheme a total stranger grasps in three seconds with zero tutorial text (taught purely by Level 1 physics), while unlocking hundreds of wild high-skill maneuvers for veterans. Your call!
